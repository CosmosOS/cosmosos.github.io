Namespace Cosmos.Kernel.System.Audio

Classes

AudioDevice

One published audio output as the ring sees it: the name the driver gave it, which driver published it, where it sits in the device tree, and its format read live from the driver. Created by Cosmos.Kernel.System.Audio.KitAudioConsumer when the kit publishes an output and kept in AudioManager's list until it is withdrawn. Once withdrawn every call is a no-op and the format reads empty; the identity members are copied at creation, so they stay readable.

AudioManager

The audio outputs the kit published, and which one is primary. Every output a kit driver publishes is listed by the manager's Cosmos.Kernel.System.Audio.KitAudioConsumer from the kit worker and dropped when it is withdrawn. Play(AudioStream) is the short way to put a stream through the primary output. Reads answer honestly before Cosmos.Kernel.System.Audio.AudioManager.Initialize() ran and when audio is compiled out: 0, null, false.

AudioPlayback

An AudioPlayer running on a thread of its own, so the caller gets on with its work while the audio plays. The thread wakes a few times per ring to top the device up and sleeps in between, which is why a kernel keeps its console, its timers and its drivers while a file plays.

Needs the scheduler running: without it there is no second thread to put this on and TryStart(AudioDevice, AudioStream, IDisposable?, out AudioPlayback?) says so, leaving the caller to play the stream inline through Play(AudioStream).

The stream is read from the playback thread from here on. A stream over a filesystem is therefore read while the rest of the kernel runs, and the filesystem layer has no locking of its own: a caller that streams off a volume should leave that volume alone until the playback ends.

AudioPlayer

Pushes an AudioStream into an AudioDevice until the stream runs out. The device owns the ring and drains it on the codec's clock; the player only keeps it fed, which is why it neither allocates per buffer nor runs from an interrupt.

Thread context: Play(AudioStream) blocks for the length of the audio, and claims the output for as long as it runs, so two players cannot interleave frames into one ring. TryStart(AudioDevice, AudioStream, IDisposable?, out AudioPlayback?) runs it on a thread of its own for a caller that must stay responsive; RequestStop() ends it early and is safe from any context.

AudioStream

A source of interleaved audio frames. The base class carries no length and no position on purpose: a stream may be endless, as a mixer or a capture device is. SeekableAudioStream adds both for the finite ones.

MemoryAudioStream

A finite stream over frames already in memory, and the RIFF/WAVE reader that builds one from a .wav file. Only uncompressed LPCM is read, which is what a .wav almost always carries.

SeekableAudioStream

A finite audio stream whose position can be read and moved.

WaveAudioStream

A RIFF/WAVE stream read straight off a volume, a block at a time. The chunk list is walked over the file handle rather than over a buffer, so nothing larger than one chunk header is held to find the frames, and a file of any size plays in a fixed amount of memory. The handle stays open for the life of the stream and belongs to whoever opened it.

Structs

WaveHeader

What the reader needs out of a RIFF/WAVE file's chunk list: the frame layout, the rate, and where the frames themselves begin and end. A .wav is a chunk list rather than a fixed header, so the chunks between fmt and data are walked rather than assumed away: a file carrying a LIST or fact chunk reads like any other.
